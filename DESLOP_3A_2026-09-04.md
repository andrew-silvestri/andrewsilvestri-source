**Ink: warm, `#241E1A`.** The palette's two hues are a cool blue and a warm
brown on a warm off-white; a cool near-black would pull the page toward the
blue and make the brown look like a mistake. Warm keeps the paper reading as
paper. **Muted: `#625C57`, not the brief's `#6B6560`.** Both pass on the
ground (5.80 vs 5.06), but muted text also sits on `--bg-lift` (code, hovered
table rows), where `#6B6560` is 4.62:1 — passing, with nothing to spare on a
cheap monitor. `#625C57` is 5.30 there.

## The door left open

- Every colour is a `:root` token; no hex in any rule, inline style or the
  remaining JS scenes is read by the stylesheet. (The hero's own colours are
  constants in `hero.js`/`hero-gl.js` — a dark picture that stays dark under
  either theme, so nothing to override.)
- The dark theme is a comment block under `:root` naming the six overrides
  it would need (`--bg #000000`, `--ink #D1D0D0`, `--dim #988686`, `--rule
  #5C4E4E`, `--acc #4E9EBC` 6.94:1, `--moss #B87A50` 5.94:1 — both verified).
  Not built.
- Figure convention, stated in the stylesheet beside `img.fig`: a figure is
  `name.png`; its dark sibling is `name-dark.png`. Honest note: swapping it
  in "without touching markup" means a theme attribute plus either a
  `<picture>` element added by a generator (the way `bust_cache.py` already
  rewrites every `<img>`), or CSS `content:` replacement. Either is a script
  change, not a hand edit of eleven pages. No page was changed for this in
  3a.
