# Phase 3d (2026-09-04): six things before the push

Not published. Item 1 is a decision; the rest are done.

## 1. The home-page globe: two renderings, and a recommendation

The correction stands: the atlas app's globe is right where it is — dark
scene, real coordinates, node lights, limb glow against the dark sea, paper
sidebar over it — and is untouched. The home page is the problem, and it is
a context problem: Part 1 dropped the frame that gave the dark sphere its
ground, so it reads as a blob with a halo on white.

Both options are built as previews, served beside the site so they use its
assets (`site/_preview/`, gitignored; `publish.sh` now deletes `_preview`
from the mirror before committing, so it cannot ship):

- **(a)** http://127.0.0.1:8765/_preview/hero-a.html — the hero on its own
  dark ground, a full-bleed band (`--sea`, the scene's ground token) the
  height of the hero, between the masthead and the main-project card. The
  globe is unchanged: same scene as the app, halo and all, which is right
  once it has its ground. Screenshot `_deslop/shots3c/hero-a.png`.
- **(b)** http://127.0.0.1:8765/_preview/hero-b.html — a light-ground
  object: the same 34,936 stations and 35,207 settlements at their recorded
  coordinates, the same coastlines, on an orthographic globe drawn on paper
  in the palette (`build_hero_figure.py` → `assets/hero_globe.png`, 338 KB;
  stations in the accent, settlements in the brown, one hairline limb, a
  faint graticule, a mono caption). No script: the page drops three.js
  (589 KB raw, ~150 on the wire), `hero.js`, `hero-gl.js` and the 109 KB
  hero payload. Screenshot `_deslop/shots3c/hero-b.png`.

**Recommendation: (b).** Three reasons. It is the same object and the same
claim — the model's real coordinates — drawn for the ground it sits on, so
the first screen reads as one page rather than a page with a panel from
another site let into it; (a)'s band is honest but a dark band under a
paper masthead is exactly the "leftover" its cost line warned of, and the
globe inside it is a 360 px sphere in a 470 px box of black. It removes
the only moving thing and 750 KB from the heaviest page, the one thing the
weight table still flags by decision. And the atlas app is one click below
it, where the moving globe earns its ground and the reader spends time.
What (a) has over it: motion, and the node lights, which the paper drawing
cannot show. If (a) is chosen, the band's height and the globe's size want
another pass so the sphere fills the band, and `hero_globe.png` comes out
of `site/assets/`.

The home page's hero is unchanged in the tree until the decision.

## 3. Marginalia: one rule

Static, everywhere. The sticky short asides and the static `.long` ones
were two behaviours for no visible reason; a note that stays put while the
prose moves is no longer beside what it annotates. The rule and its reason
are in `style.css` beside `aside.marginalia`; `.long` is left on the markup
and does nothing. I agree with the argument, and add one: sticky asides
were also why the marginalia painted over figures on 2026-08-30 and again
on 09-04 — a static note is in the flow and the grid can keep it off
everything. Layout suite 44/44 after.

## 4. Skyline labels

Two collisions, both real. The meter's bars ran under the notes line
because the canvas's baseline was 30 px from the bottom and the foot (two
lines of 12 px with padding) is 60; it is 66 now. Tower names printed
through the compass points because the collision test only knew about
other tower names; every piece of text the canvas draws is now a box in
one list, the names sit a line below the compass ring, and a name that
would overlap anything is dropped. `test_app.js` records every `fillText`
as a box (with `measureText` sized from the string, so the app's own test
runs on the same numbers) and fails if any two overlap, every city at
eight bearings, and if any bar reaches into the foot — 43/43, the way
`test_layout.js` does it for the marginalia.

## 5. One node, one step

Rebuilt through `sitefig.py` as a worked example: three drivers with
effects and weights into the node, four stages each with its operation,
its arithmetic in mono, and the running value, an applied change from
above, and the node's new effect. The numbers are made up and the footnote
says so; the arithmetic is the engine's, read from `atlas-app.js` (the
step fraction 0.95 is parsed from the source so it cannot drift), and it
shows `(1 − 0.6·inertia)` because that is what the code does — the prose
still says `(1 − inertia)` and is item 3 of the claims list. No title, no
section tag, boxes square on `--card`, PROSE width at 2.7:1. The audit now
also checks that what a box holds fits inside it, because the first render
had three titles through their edges without a single text-on-text
overlap. `CARD_FILL` added to `sitefig.py` for a box on a diagram.
