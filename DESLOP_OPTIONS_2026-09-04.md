## 2. The ceiling: what styling cannot fix

Stated before the options, as asked. These are findings from the audit that
no stylesheet resolves; each option below says which it *touches*, none
claims to close them.

- **F3 page weight.** Out of the options by Amendment 2; written up in
  `DESLOP_WEIGHT_2026-09-04.md`. Every option's home page is still ~1.5 MB
  because the same nine oversized images load. The webfont options add
  57–109 KB on a first visit, cached thereafter; that is paid for several
  times over by the first item in the weight doc.
- **F4 figures illegible at 390 px.** The figures are PNGs with 10–11 pt
  labels rendered at 0.16–0.33 scale. CSS can only give them more of a
  350 px column than they already have. The real fix is a phone-sized
  render from each builder, or SVG output; the partial fix is a lightbox
  that allows pinch-zoom at natural size (`lightbox.js`, JS not CSS).
- **F5, the cross-media half.** A stylesheet can load one face for the
  page. It cannot change the Segoe/DejaVu in 47 PNG figures or the
  Computer Modern in five videos; those need the builders re-run with the
  chosen face (matplotlib can load a TTF by path) and the Manim scenes
  re-rendered. Options A and B make the page consistent with itself; the
  figures will still be in a different face until that pass.
- **F6, the deletion half.** Removing or converting the margin scenes,
  sys-log and force graph is a markup change (script tags, inline config)
  on every page that carries them, not a CSS one. The previews make those
  markup changes on their three pages; Phase 3 would make them on all
  eleven. `data-motion="off"` already stops them, which is why the CSS
  alone cannot: they are `aria-hidden` canvases, not styled elements.
- **F8** was JS (done in the baseline). **F11** (apps) is Phase 3 chrome by
  the brief, and the longevity-app's Georgia body and 1,036 px measure are
  its own inline CSS. **F10** (title suffixes, model.html's Title Case,
  em-dash vs spaced hyphen) is copy mechanics under the punctuation
  exception; I have not touched any page's copy and will not without a
  word from you. **F12** phone nav is nav structure, not styling.
- **Not mine:** the longevity video's "45×" disagreeing with the table
  (revamp pass 5), and the six orphaned assets.

## 3. The three options

The audit's findings cluster in three places: the identity and structure
of the home page (F2, F7, F12); type and its consistency (F5, F10); and
the decoration layer (F6, F9). Each option takes a different position on
the decoration layer, as the go-ahead asked, and a different position on
type; all three make the same home-page structural change (an `h1` and a
standfirst, see §4) because that is a defect, not a direction.

### Option A — "The report"

*The article pages are already a paper. The whole site becomes one.*

- **Type.** Self-hosted **Source Serif 4** (400, 400 italic, 600) for
  everything a reader reads, **Source Sans 3** (400, 600) for UI, tables,
  captions and references. One six-step scale (12.5 / 14.5 / 18 / 20 / 24
  / 38 px); body 18 px on 1.6; measure 66ch. `h2` becomes a 24 px serif
  sentence-case heading with one rule under it; `th` sentence case; no
  uppercase tracked text anywhere.
- **Decoration: deleted.** No washes, no node tile, no margin scenes, no
  sys-log, no force graph, no cards. The hero stays (it is the model).
  Cards become entries: a rule above, kicker in plain sans, title, figure,
  text. The mosaic strip stays but loses its frames.
- **Markup changes (preview copies only):** masthead inserted before the
  hero on index; `<ul class="sys-log">`, `syslog.js`, `margin-scene.js`
  and its inline config removed on index and heat; `force-graph-data.js`,
  `force-bg.js` and `class="bg-force-graph"` removed on model. In Phase 3
  the same on the other eight pages, plus `biome-scene.js` on longevity —
  which is the one deletion I would argue against (audit §5); A as drawn
  deletes it for consistency and you can overrule.
- **Resolves:** F1 (baseline), F2 (with §4), F5 page half, F6, F7, F9. A
  page's `hr`+`h2` double rule is gone (the `hr` is hidden; the `h2`
  carries the rule).
- **Leaves open:** F4, F5 figure/video half, F10, F11, F12; and the site
  loses its second CDN dependency (force-graph) only by deleting the
  graph, which A does.
- **Costs:** +109 KB fonts on first visit. The most change to the
  vocabulary: the violet kickers, the boxed cards and the ambient graph
  all go, so it is the option most likely to read as "a different site"
  to someone who knows the current one. Idle CPU on article pages drops
  from ~300 ms/3 s to ~0.
- **What it looks like:** `_deslop/shots/opt/a-*.png`. The model page is
  now a report with a proper serif; the heat page reads like a set paper
  with figures as plates.

### Option B — "The instrument"

*The site reads like the atlas app's own panel: a humanist sans for prose,
a mono for every number, and the margin scenes converted into text you can
read.*

- **Type.** Self-hosted **IBM Plex Sans** (400, 400 italic, 600) for prose
  and headings, **IBM Plex Mono** (400) for tables, code, kickers,
  citations, the readout and the marginalia. Body 16.5 px on 1.62; measure
  68ch. `h2` is a 16 px semibold sentence-case heading with a rule *above*
  it; `th` mono sentence case. No serif anywhere on the page.
- **Decoration: converted, not deleted.** This is the option that takes the
  six revamp passes at their word. The `MARGIN_SCENE` numbers on each page
  become two `<aside class="marginalia">` lists in the left and right
  gutters, 12.5 px mono, sticky so they follow the reader (see
  `b-heat-desk.png`: "gas: 5.13 dollars/MMBtu … electric fuel: $19.44 of
  $20.00" beside the paragraph that says so). The sys-log becomes a
  readout strip: same four lines, mono, ruled above and below, no `$`
  prompt, no cursor, and no longer `aria-hidden`. The canvases behind text
  (margin scenes, force graph, node tile, washes) go; the hero stays.
  Cards become rows: figure at 38 % on the left, words on the right, a
  rule between rows.
- **Markup changes (preview copies only):** masthead before the hero;
  `aria-hidden` and the cursor span removed from the sys-log; the inline
  `MARGIN_SCENE` config parsed into the two asides (labels only, verbatim,
  nothing computed) and the scene script removed; force graph removed on
  model. Phase 3 would do the same conversion on library, storage,
  climate-cost, skyline and desktop, and decide what longevity's biome
  scene becomes (its labels are species + quotient + lifespan, which
  convert the same way).
- **Resolves:** F1, F2, F5 page half, F6 (by making the numbers legible:
  12.5 px at full contrast instead of 10 px at 40 %), F7, F9.
- **Leaves open:** F4, F5 figure half (Plex Sans is close to the figures'
  Segoe, so the mismatch is smallest here), F10, F11, F12.
- **Costs:** +57–79 KB fonts. On the phone the marginalia cannot sit in a
  gutter, so it renders as a two-column list before the content
  (`b-index-phone.png`); on the home page that is 20 items ahead of the
  hero, which is too many and would be trimmed or collapsed in Phase 3.
  The marginalia spans the grid rows beside it (`grid-row: span 60`) so it
  never pushes content down; that is a real CSS pattern but an unusual one
  and worth knowing about before it ships. B is also the option that
  changes the site's voice most: serif to sans.
- **What it looks like:** `_deslop/shots/opt/b-*.png`.

*The vocabulary is fine; the execution was not. Keep the dark ground, the

  a 20 px italic — the one display flourish. `h2` keeps the uppercase
  tracked voice at 12.5 px; `th` at 12 px; carets and cites at 11 px.
- **Decoration: kept as texture, stripped of words.** The washes, the node
  tile and the force graph stay; the ground lifts slightly to navy

- **Cards kept, corrected:** flat card fill (no gradient, no inset

  section boundaries; card grid always `minmax(300px, 1fr)`.
- **Markup changes (preview copies only):** masthead inserted *after* the
  hero on index; sys-log, `syslog.js`, margin-scene script and config

- **Resolves:** F1, F2, F6 by half (the illegible words go, the ambience

### 3a. Fonts against F3: is the comparison fair?

Yes, and the suspicion is right: the font cost is close to irrelevant
next to the image cost, for a specific reason that is worth stating with
the numbers.

- **What a font costs.** A: 109 KB (five files); B: 57 KB on most pages,
  79 KB on a page that uses italic; C: 0. Fetched on the first page of a
  visit only. Every later page in the same visit reads them from cache,
  because the same five URLs are referenced from every page.
- **What a figure costs.** Each figure is unique to its page, so nothing
  is shared: index 1,231 KB of images, longevity 977, library 903,
  storage 622, heat 476. A reader who opens the home page and one project
  page downloads 1.7–2.2 MB of images and, under A, 109 KB of fonts once.
  Across all eleven pages the image total is ~5.6 MB; the whole Source
  set is 2 % of that. `lq_allometry.png` alone (237 KB) is twice the font
  set.
- **Caching in practice.** The live site serves everything with
  `Cache-Control: max-age=600` and an ETag (checked against
  andrewsilvestri.com today). So within ten minutes the fonts cost
  nothing; after ten minutes the browser revalidates with
  `If-None-Match` and gets a 304 of a few hundred bytes, unless the file
  changed. That is the same regime the figures live under, so the
  comparison is like for like. One housekeeping note for Phase 3:
  `bust_cache.py` stamps `style.css`, scripts and images but not files
  referenced from inside CSS, so a changed font must get a new filename;
  the subset names carry no version today.
- **The weight doc's first item pays for it.** Thumbnail renders for the
  home page save ~600 KB on `index.html` alone, five to six times the
  font set. Items 2–4 (lazy loading, palette PNGs, WebP posters) save
  another ~1 MB across the site.
- **Where it is not irrelevant:** the first paint of a first visit on a
  slow connection. 109 KB is about one second at 1 Mbit/s, during which
  `font-display: swap` shows the fallback face, then reflows. Under A the
  fallback (Palatino/Iowan) has different metrics from Source Serif 4, so
  the reflow is visible; under B, Plex Sans against `system-ui` is a
  smaller shift. If that second matters, the fix is a `<link
  rel="preload">` for the two body files, not a different option.

So: the fair comparison is 57–109 KB once against 0.6–1.2 MB per page,
and the option choice should not turn on font bytes.

### 3b. Option C and F5: what it does and does not close

Stated directly, because C is the option a reader could pick under a
misapprehension.

F5 has two halves. **The scale half** (15 rendered sizes at half-pixel
steps, three sub-12 px roles on every page, uppercase tracked text in
three sizes) C closes as fully as A or B: 7–9 sizes per page, nothing
under 11 px, one kicker size. **The face half** C does not close, and my
earlier line "no longer falls to nothing on Android" overstated what
pinning the stack does:

- Before C, the stack `Iowan Old Style, Palatino Linotype, Palatino,
  Georgia, Times New Roman, serif` resolves to **Palatino Linotype on
  Windows, Iowan Old Style on macOS and iOS, and the generic `serif` on
  Android and Linux**, which Android maps to Noto Serif and Linux to
  whatever fontconfig prefers (usually DejaVu Serif or Liberation Serif).
- Under C, adding `Charter, "Bitstream Charter", "Noto Serif"` to the list
  changes nothing on Windows or Apple platforms (Palatino Linotype and
  Iowan are still hit first), names Noto Serif explicitly on Android
  (which the generic already reached), and offers Charter on the few
  Linux installs that have it. The practical effect is that the
  Android and Linux face is now *chosen* rather than *defaulted*; it is
  not the same face as anywhere else.
- So under C the body face is still **three faces by OS**, the figures
  are still Segoe/DejaVu, the videos are still Computer Modern, and
  longevity-app is still Georgia. Nothing on the site shares a face with
  anything else on the site. That is the finding, and C leaves it open.
- What C buys instead: zero font bytes, zero first-paint reflow, and no
  new files to maintain. What it forgoes: the one property every
  reference site in the audit had, a face that was paid for and used
  everywhere.

If you want C's restraint *and* F5 closed, the honest hybrid is C's
override block plus A's or B's `@font-face` declarations; that is a
fourth option and I have not built it, but it is a ten-line change to
`_deslop/build_previews.py` if you ask.
