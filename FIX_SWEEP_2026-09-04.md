## 1. The bookshelf wording: held for a decision

Option A, read against the shipped `site/bookshelf-app.html` rather than the
note it was drafted in, is overstated in one clause. The line:

> "The optional cover mode sends each book's ISBN to Open Library, and
> nothing else."

What the shipped code does (`fetch(url, {credentials: "omit",
referrerPolicy: "no-referrer"})`, one request per book that has an ISBN): the
request carries the ISBN in the URL and nothing else *from the export* — no
title, rating, shelf, cookie or referrer. It also carries what every HTTP
request carries, the reader's address and browser string, and it follows Open
Library's redirect to Internet Archive download hosts. The note's own
`desktop.html` paragraph scopes the claim ("nothing else from it"); A drops
the scope. Second, smaller: "each book's ISBN" — a book with no ISBN in the
export is never looked up, and the panel counts those separately.

A is not understated: "optional" is true of a box that loads unticked and
unticks itself on every file load, though it says less than the design does.

Nothing was changed. The three places stand as they are:

- `index.html` card: "A wallpaper built from a Goodreads export: your books as
  drawn spines, or as a wall of their real covers brought to one finish. The
  CSV is parsed in the browser and never leaves it."
- `desktop.html` card: "The page works with the network cable out. Tick *Real
  covers* and it sends each book's ISBN to Open Library to fetch its cover —
  the one thing it ever sends anywhere, and only when you ask." (Its long
  paragraph carries the scoped version.)
- `bookshelf/README.md` (and so the zip): "Ticking it sends one request per
  book to Open Library (an Internet Archive project) carrying that book's ISBN
  from your export and nothing else from it - no title, no rating, no cookie,
  no referrer."

Once the wording is decided, it goes into `site/index.html`,
`site/desktop.html` (card and paragraph) and `bookshelf/README.md`, then
`python rezip_downloads.py`, and `tests/test_generators.py` confirms the zip
agrees.

## 8. The cards were cropped, and the CSS was not where the crop was

The cards use the twelve `-thumb.png` files and the thumbnails are 3:2 —
both true — and `sitefig.thumbnail()` made them by centre cover-cropping
figures of 1.60–2.10:1 (and one of 0.88:1) to 3:2. So the crop was in the
asset: the skyline chart lost 13% of its width each side, the tomato chain
29% in all, the ranking 41% of its height. The card's `object-fit: cover`
fitted a 3:2 image to a 3:2 box and hid nothing, which is why nothing in
the CSS looked wrong. A wiring problem in the sense the brief meant: the
Part 2 note said the thumbnails were rendered at 3:2 "so nothing is
cropped", and rendered-at-3:2 meant cropped-to-3:2.

Fixed at the source. `sitefig.thumbnail()` scales the whole figure to 542px
wide at its own aspect; `build_thumbnails.py` lists the twelve and is in the
generator check. Cards drop the fixed aspect and `object-fit` and size to
the image (the ranking's card is the tall one, 542×615 at full width). The
mosaic keeps a tile, 16:9 to fit the six WIDE library figures within 2%,
with `object-fit: contain` on the figure's own ground — the original rule's
warning ("a centred cover-crop was cutting whole panels and axis labels
off") is back in the comment, with the account of how it was lost.

Dead space: the card grid now has rows `1fr auto auto auto 1fr` with the
image spanning all five and centred; whichever column is taller sets the
height and the other floats mid-way. Measured after: gap under the image
and under the text are split above and below rather than dumped at the
foot (before: 20–76px under five images, 36px under one text).

"New York": at 1920 the gutter is still 192px (the shell is capped and the
extra width goes to the outer margins), no marginalia element is wider than
its box, and "cheese, France to New York" wraps at the space before "York"
(151px + 29px lines). Wrapping, not clipping. `text-wrap: pretty` on the
items lets the engine avoid the one-word last line. `test_layout.js` now
runs 1920 as well and fails on any marginalia element whose scrollWidth
exceeds its box, so the two cannot be confused again.
