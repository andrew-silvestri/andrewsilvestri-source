## What was built, and how honestly

- **Sheets.** `site/style-palette-1.css` … `-4.css` are the corrected
  `style.css` with its gradients stripped (see the amendment below) plus one override block that redefines the colour
  tokens for both OS schemes, turns off the two radial washes and the
  node tile (both are literals in the old palette), and adds the stale
  marker styles. Structure and type are the current baseline, not options
  A, B or C. Generated by `_deslop/build_palettes.py` from
  `_deslop/palettes.py`.
- **Figures regenerated, 19 per palette,** into
  `site/_preview/palette-<n>/assets/` — nothing shipped was overwritten:
  the 12 library figures (`build_atlas_figures.py`), the home overview
  chart (`build_model_chart.py`), the throughlines sheet and the
  propagation step on `model.html`, and all five heat figures. Method:
  `_deslop/fig_palettes.py` copies each builder, rewrites every one of the
  30 site hex literals to the palette's colour for that role
  (`palettes.SITE_HEX_ROLES`), and redirects `OUT`. Every regenerated
  figure passed its builder's own layout audit (0 problems, all four
  runs). About 20 s per palette.
- **Figures not regenerated are dimmed to 45 % with a dashed outline and
  a label** ("Not regenerated for this palette · shown dimmed · name").
  Home: `storage_fig3`, `climate_chain_tomato`, `lq_ranked`, and the two
  video posters. Library: `14_space_layer` (a different builder). Model:
  none. You cannot mistake them for the design.
- **JS scenes.** The hero (`hero.js` / `hero-gl.js`) is left running in
  all four: its node colours are constants in two files, so it is a dark
  picture in a frame on the three light palettes, and on Gothic it is the
  only colour on the page. Both facts are part of the judgement below.
  `margin-scene.js` and `syslog.js` read their colours from the CSS
  tokens, so they are kept on Gothic and removed from the three light
  palettes (a 40 % alpha canvas tuned for black is noise on paper).
  `force-bg.js` has its own hard-coded kind colours and is removed from
  all four. `biome-scene.js` is not on any of the three pages.
- **Measured** (`_deslop/results-palette.json`): 24 runs, **0 AA
  failures** on any text pair, dark or light scheme. Every ratio is in
  `_deslop/palette/contrast.md` and reproduced in full at the end of this
  file. Deuteranopia simulation (Machado 2009, severity 1.0) of each
  palette's overview chart is in `shots/palette/p<n>-chart-deuteranopia.png`
  with `p0-` for the current violet chart.

A note on the "FAIL" rows in the full tables: WCAG sets 4.5:1 for text
and 3:1 for a graphic against its ground. It sets nothing for a rule
against the ground (the current site's rule is 1.4:1) or for one series
against another, which is separability rather than legibility. Those rows
are printed against a 3:1 line because the brief asked for every pair;
none of them is a shipping blocker, and the series-vs-series ones are the
useful part of the diagnosis.

## 1. Frozen lake — `#6D8196` slate · `#ADD8E6` icy blue · `#FFFAFA` snow · `#000080` navy

**Tokens.** Snow is the ground, navy is ink *and* accent (links, kickers,
buttons with snow text), icy blue is the rule colour and, at half
strength (`#D6E8F0`), the card surface. Slate could not be used as given:
3.88:1 on snow fails body text, so muted text is a darkened slate
`#4E6076` (6.23:1). A mid-blue `#3F5F8A` was added for code, hover and the
second data series, and a rust `#B5563B` was imported for the one role
the palette cannot fill: negative weights, earthquakes, "event".

**Contrast, text pairs.** ink 15.48 · muted 6.23 · links 15.48 · code
5.96 · button 15.48 · hover 6.32 · on card: ink 12.7, muted 5.11, kicker 12.7.
All pass. Series against ground: navy 15.5, mid-blue 6.3, slate 3.9, rust
4.7, and two of the derived tints (`#8FA6BA`, `#7FB3CC`) fall under 3:1 —
the icy blue cannot be a line on snow at any strength that still reads as
icy.

**What had to change.** Four things, listed above; the given slate survives
only as one series and the nav hover. The palette as printed has two
readable colours and two surfaces, exactly as the brief predicted.

**What it does to the site.** The home page becomes a clean, cold,
institutional page — navy text on near-white with an ice-blue card — and
reads immediately as a university department or a 1990s default link
colour, which is a kind of authority but not this site's kind. The hero
turns into a framed dark photograph on a white wall; it stops being the
ground the site stands on. The figures are the problem: with one hue, the
overview chart's twelve kinds are a ramp of blues plus one rust, and
"Fuel supply", "National grid" and "Consumer group" become
indistinguishable bars (see `p1-chart-deuteranopia.png`; it is no worse
for a deuteranope because there was nothing to lose). A model whose whole
argument is that different kinds of thing behave differently cannot be
drawn in one hue. Frozen lake would suit a site about one thing; this is
a site about twelve.

## 2. Yacht club — `#F2F0EF` off-white · `#BBBDBC` grey · `#245F73` deep blue · `#733E24` brown

**Tokens.** Off-white ground, a near-white card, the grey as rule and
faint. Ink is not one of the four: the palette has no dark neutral, and
body text in the deep blue at 6.25:1 reads as coloured text on every
page, so ink is a near-black mixed from the brown (`#2B211C`, 13.8:1),
which keeps the warmth. Deep blue is the accent (links, kickers, buttons
with white at 7.1:1); brown is the second data colour, which turns out to
be a good semantic — brown for gas and fuel, blue for electricity, on the
heat figures. Muted text is a darkened grey (`#5F6261`, 5.43:1) because
the given grey is 1.66:1. Series beyond the two hues are tints of them.

**Contrast, text pairs.** ink 13.83 · muted 5.43 · links 6.25 · code 5.71 ·
button 7.10 · hover 9.65 · on card: ink 14.94, muted 5.86, kicker 6.75. All
pass. Series against ground: blue 6.25, brown 7.57, tints 3.4–3.6, the
two lightest tints 2.1–2.8. Blue against brown is 1.21:1 — isoluminant,
so adjacent series are told apart by hue only, which is fine for normal
vision (blue/brown is not a confusion pair) and survives deuteranopia as
blue/khaki (`p2-chart-deuteranopia.png`).

**What had to change.** The ink and the muted grey, both derived; the
tints. Nothing imported.

**What it does to the site.** This is the one that looks designed. Warm
paper, a teal that is not the web's default blue, a brown nobody would
pick by accident, and the serif body already on the site suddenly reads
as print rather than as "dark mode serif". The library page is the test
and it passes: single-series figures in teal on paper look like a
well-set report (`p2-library-desk.png`). The home page becomes a magazine
front rather than a dashboard. The costs are real: the hero is a dark
plate on a warm page, which works as a picture but loses the "the site is
the model" feeling; the six videos would be dark islands until
re-rendered; and two hues still cannot separate twelve kinds — the
overview chart needs the tint ramp and its legend is harder to read than
the violet original's. If the site is going to a light ground, this is
the palette to start from.

## 3. Gothic noir — `#000000` black · `#D1D0D0` light grey · `#988686` taupe · `#5C4E4E` dark taupe

**Tokens.** Black ground (pure, not the site's navy-black), light grey
ink, taupe for muted text and for the accent — links, kickers and the
button fill, with black text on it at 6.09:1 — and dark taupe as the
rule and the card border. Muted text on the card surface is kept at the
light grey because taupe on dark taupe is 2.29:1. Three greys were added
between the given taupes for the series ramp, and one hue was imported:
oxblood `#C4515F` for negative weights, earthquakes and "event".

**Contrast, text pairs.** ink 13.64 · muted 6.09 · links 6.09 · code 12.67 ·
button 6.09 · hover 9.31 · on card: ink 12.0, muted 5.36, kicker 5.36.
All pass. Series against ground: the greys 4.7–13.6, dark taupe 2.65 (it
is a fill for the smallest kind), oxblood 4.69. Every series against
every other series is 1.3–2.9 because they differ only in lightness.

**Can a data site use this palette without importing a colour?** No.
Stated plainly: the palette is achromatic, and this site encodes twelve
node kinds, positive and negative link weights, gas against electric,
wild against captive, and economic against mass allocation in hue. On
the overview chart (`palette-3/assets/energy_model_chart.png`) the
twelve kinds are a lightness ramp and the eight-entry legend has three
entries a reader cannot tell apart. One import — the oxblood, for the
things that are warnings — makes it usable as a greyscale-with-one-alarm
system, which is a legitimate editorial style. Two imports (a cold blue
as well) would be the honest minimum for the figures, and at that point
the palette is no longer Gothic noir.

**What it does to the site.** It is the cheapest conversion by far — the
ground stays dark, the hero and the videos stay as they are, the margin
scenes retint themselves from the tokens — and it is the most coherent
of the four as a *page*: black, grey, one warm grey, nothing shouting.
The model page reads like a well-printed negative. But the hero becomes
the only coloured object on the site (violet and blue on black in a
frame of greys), and every figure loses the thing figures are for. It
also lands squarely on the "permanent dark mode" tell without the
counter-argument the violet baseline has, which is that its colour is
doing semantic work. Austere, funereal, and wrong for a site whose
subject is how different things behave differently.

## 4. Quite clear — `#CBCBCB` grey · `#F2F2F2` off-white · `#174D38` deep green · `#4D1717` maroon

**Tokens.** Off-white ground, the grey as rule and faint, and — because
the palette offers no dark neutral — **the deep green is the body ink**.
Maroon is the accent: links, kickers, and buttons with white at 14.48:1.
Muted text is a derived grey (`#5F5F5F`, 5.7:1; the given grey is
1.45:1). For the figures the second series is a *lifted* green
(`#3E8A63`), not the deep green, so that it sits 3.46:1 from the maroon
in lightness.

**Contrast, text pairs.** ink 8.70 · muted 5.70 · links 12.93 · code 6.11 ·
button 14.48 · hover 11.04 · on card: ink 9.34, muted 6.12, kicker 13.87.
All pass. Series against ground: maroon 12.9, deep green 8.7, lifted green
3.74, lighter maroon 6.69, greys 3.8 and 2.2. Deep green against maroon is
1.49:1, as the brief said.

**Deuteranopia, checked rather than asserted.** `p4-chart-deuteranopia.png`:
maroon and deep green both collapse to the same olive; they are not
distinguishable by hue at all. What survives is the lightness step I
built in — lifted green against maroon reads as light olive against dark
olive, so the bar chart and the donut still separate, and the
"positive/negative" histogram still reads. With the palette used as
given (deep green and maroon as adjacent series) it would not. So the
palette can be made safe, but only by never using its two hues at their
given darkness next to each other, which is most of what a two-hue
palette is for.

**What it does to the site.** Green ink is the whole character. Two
thousand words of body text in `#174D38` is legible at 8.7:1 and reads,
after a paragraph, like a form filled in with a green pen; every page
looks the same shade of institutional — a club, a bank, a college with a
crest. The maroon accent sharpens that impression rather than cutting
it. On the library page the figures in maroon-and-green are handsome one
at a time and monotonous as a set (`p4-library-desk-full.png`). It is the
palette here most likely to be called "designed" by someone glancing at
it and least likely to be read through. A green-ink site can exist; a
green-ink site with thirty data figures and a colour-vision hazard
between its only two hues should not.

## Which one I would argue for, and whether any beats the baseline

**None of the four beats the corrected violet baseline for this site.**
The reason is not taste. The site's colour has a job: violet, moss, blue
and rose separate twelve node kinds, sign, fuel against electricity,
wild against captive, and economic against mass allocation, and every
one of thirty-four figures, six videos, five apps and four JS scenes is
already drawn in it. Each trial palette has at most two usable hues
(Yacht club, Quite clear), one (Frozen lake) or none (Gothic noir), and
each needed two to four derived colours before it could carry body text
at all — which is the "stock palette adopted unchanged" problem the brief
warned about, arriving from the other direction: they cannot be adopted
unchanged, and once changed they are no longer the palette on the list.
The audit did not find the violet to be the site's problem; it found
that the violet was well used and the type, home page and decoration
were not. Changing the palette would spend a day of conversion, leave
the videos dark until re-rendered from a source this repo does not hold,
and turn the hero into a picture on a wall, to fix something that
measured as fine.

**If a light ground is wanted anyway, argue for Yacht club** and treat
it as a starting point: keep its off-white, teal and brown, keep the
brown-derived ink, and add one cold and one warm data colour so the
figures have four hues again. That is a real design decision rather than
a swatch, and it is the only one of the four that made the library page
look better than it does now.

**Gothic noir** is worth naming as the cheapest thing that could be
done, because it would take an afternoon — but it should not be done,
because it deletes the information the figures carry.
