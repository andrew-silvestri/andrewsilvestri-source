## A1. The hero

### What the brief got wrong first

The layer diagram was not "never built". `build_layer_diagram.py` existed and
`atlas_layers.png` was live above the atlas table. It was not the figure the
audit asked for, and it was not honest about the payload:

- It hand-typed thirteen kind-level links. The payload carries twenty-five
  kind pairs. Missing: event→station (23,320 edges), event→consumer
  (22,025), grid→consumer (34,006), market→grid (2,855), event→district
  (3,436), and three of two edges or fewer. The two biggest one-way links in
  the model were not on the picture of the model.
- Nine boxes scattered on a 2:1 sheet with a blank top third, nine per-layer
  colours (the same over-stretched palette A3 complains of), and one render
  at 1140 that no phone could read.

So it was rewritten rather than moved.

### What was built

`build_layer_diagram.py`, three renders through `sitefig.py`:

- **Bands.** Nine, one per layer in the atlas table's grouping, name and
  node count. Counts are sums over the payload's kinds, and the script fails
  if they differ from what `update_atlas_pages.py` writes into the table.
- **Arcs.** Every layer-to-layer link the payload has, counted kind by kind
  and folded to layers. One-way links on the right with one head, two-way on
  the left with two, the stroke the number of edges the arc stands for on a
  log scale (1 to 66,264). The rank table is parsed out of `atlas-app.js`,
  the engine of record, the way the propagation figure parses the step
  fraction. The build fails if any cross-rank edge in the payload runs from
  a higher rank to a lower one, or if two kind-level edges between the same
  two layers disagree about direction.
- **The rule, drawn.** A dashed line between Events and Demand. Every arc
  crossing it has one head and points down. That is HANDOFF §6's rank rule
  as a picture.
- **Three widths.** 1140 for the wide track on atlas.html, 714 for the home
  page's measure, 350 for a phone (390 minus main's padding), each drawn at
  its display width so nothing is scaled. `sitefig.PHONE` and
  `fig_floor.KNOWN_DISPLAY` know the new width. A `<picture>` switches to
  the phone render under 760px; `bust_cache.py` now stamps `srcset`.
- **Audit.** Text overlap, off-canvas, the 12px floor, text-in-band, arc
  reach, and the band order: the audit tries all 120 orders of the block
  below the rule and requires the drawn one to reach the minimum number of
  crossings. The minimum is one, and it is forced: Climate and Events each
  drive both Grids and Markets, while Space and Weather drive only Grids, so
  whichever of Grids and Markets comes first, one arc cuts another. The
  figure says so in its foot. The order that reaches it and keeps Grids in
  the middle of the block it is the hub of is Demand, Power plants, Markets
  and fuel, Grids, Behaviour.
- **The table follows the figure** (your item 4). `update_atlas_pages.py`
  imports `LAYERS` from the diagram and orders its rows by it, and the
  caption under the figure says the order is the one that minimises
  crossings, so nobody puts it back.
- **No count bars** (your item 3): the model chart one scroll below already
  draws node counts per kind on a log scale.
- **In the generator check.** `tests/test_generators.py` runs it into a copy
  and diffs the three PNGs against `site/`; Agg is deterministic here and
  the check passed twice.

Wired: the home page's hero is the 714 render with a one-line caption naming
the generator; atlas.html carries the 1140 render above its table, with a
caption that names the generator, the rank rule and the order. The globe is
under the atlas intro, with its own caption naming `build_hero_figure.py`.

## A3. The model chart

1. **Soft.** Not the last figure on the old geometry. It is drawn at 714
   (the geometry is right) and was displayed at 1140 by `.fig.wide` on a
   page that had gone two-track the night before. Upscaled 1.6x. So were
   all twelve card and mosaic thumbnails on the page: 271px renders shown
   at 433 and 374. Fixed by A4 (the page is one 714px measure again), and
   the chart's class is `.fig`. Every figure on every page is now displayed
   at its render width, measured at 1440, with one exception found and left:
   `climate_allocation_bases.png` is drawn at 1140 and shown at 714 on a
   notes page, its labels at 9px. That is a Part D item, noted here.
2. **Legend on panel label.** Real, and the audit missed it for a reason
   that is bigger than this figure: `sitefig.panel()` sets a left-aligned
   title, matplotlib keeps that in `ax._left_title`, and every audit on the
   site read `ax.title`, the centre slot, so no audit anywhere had ever seen
   a panel label. `sitefig.titles(ax)` returns all three slots; five audits
   (throughlines, allocation chain, atlas figures, longevity explained,
   climate-cost allocation) now use it. The audit also treats a legend as a
   box: its whole extent is checked against every other text, because the
   first fix put a colour key over the "18" of insolation with no text
   touching text. The chart's upper row is taller and the gap wider; the
   legend clears.
3. **Panel titles in two faces.** Not reproducible: the shipped PNG is
   byte-identical to a fresh build and all four labels are mono. What was
   true is that three panels on the atlas figures (`11_climate_record`,
   `14_space_layer`) were still set with `ax.set_title()`, in sans, inside
   the pass that made the rule. They go through `panel()` now, and
   `sitefig.save()` refuses any figure carrying an axes title that did not
   come through `panel()`, so it cannot recur in any builder that saves
   through sitefig. (The heat and storage builders have their own
   `figstyle.py` and do not use panels.)

**Colour.** Twelve kinds in one hue each was too many. The bars now carry
the one distinction the model makes between kinds, the same one the hero
draws: the ranked kinds (`atlas-app.js`'s RANK, read from the source) in the
one-way slate, the rest in the two-way blue, with a two-entry key under the
axes. The donut's source names are no longer cut at 26 characters.

## A4. Two left edges

**Decision: the home page is one measure.** `main.notes`, the same grid the
seven marginalia pages use, with nothing in the gutters. Why: a page with
empty gutters has no reason to break out of its measure; every figure and
thumbnail on the page was drawn for that 714px column (sitefig NOTES and
THUMB) and had gone soft the day the page left it; and the hero at 714 sits
on the measure, which is the alternative to dropping the `.plain` cap (your
item 2). Measured at 1440: every block from the masthead to the footer at
363..1077. At 390 everything at 20..370.

**The rule, and where it bent.** Text blocks share one left edge; a picture
may break out only centred on the measure. On the prose pages (atlas, model,
library, code) the tables were being promoted to the wide track with the
figures, which is exactly two edges; `main > table` is no longer promoted,
and every table on the site fits the measure by wrapping its prose column
(measured: no table scrolls at 756). Wide figures on those pages stay as
centred breakouts, because at 756 a 1140 render's labels fall under the
floor. library.html's four text blocks are `.flush`: flush to the wide edge
its cards use, held to the measure. `tests/test_layout.js` now fails a page
whose text blocks have more than one left edge, or whose breakout picture
is off-centre: 44/44.

**Card ratio.** 38% is kept and now written down: on the 714 measure it is
271px, the thumbnails' own width, so a card never scales its picture; the
text column is 415px, about 48 characters of the small face. Image heights
129 to 308, text heights 149 to 233, the shorter centred on the other.

## A4, second pass: the card spec

Four passes had each fixed one card symptom and revealed the next - the
crop, the 3:2 aspect, the dead space, the alignment - because nothing said
what a card is. Measured before touching anything, at 1440: six cards, five
heights (219 to 357px); the image floating in five of them, with 43px of
air inside the padding on skyline, 57 on the bookshelf, 5 on longevity
(where the 0.88 image set the height instead); and 5px more air above than
below on every card. The cause was as stated: native aspect, adopted to stop
the crop, guaranteed the thumbnails could never share a shape. The numbers
in the brief were about 35px high; the diagnosis was right.

**The spec** is written in `style.css` beside `.cardgrid .card` ("THE CARD
SPEC") and summarised in HANDOFF §4, and the code follows it: one 4:3 canvas
for every card thumbnail, 542 x 406 bitmap, the figure fitted whole on its
own ground sampled from its corners (the mosaic's rule, now in the asset
via `sitefig.thumbnail(aspect=4/3)`); a fixed 271px image column - not 38%,
because the measure is fluid and 38% shrank the image under the text at
1024; 28px gutter; 22px padding; the image sets the height, 247px plus its
rule, every card; the text is a budget of a one-line title and five lines,
centred when shorter, a test failure when longer. Below 760 the card stacks
in source order: tag, title, image, paragraph.

**Implementing to it** cost two copy edits and one title: the skyline and
bookshelf paragraphs were six and seven lines, and "Industrial heat
electrification break-even" wrapped to two lines at the 1024 measure; it is
now the nav's own name, "Industrial heat break-even". The bookshelf's
cover-mode sentence was first shortened, which lost its scope ("nothing
else from your export", the Internet Archive redirect, "optional"): the
same trim that produced a false statement once before. It is dropped from
the card; desktop.html states it in full, twice. The spec now says the copy
serves the card and that a scoped claim is dropped, never compressed.

**The test.** `tests/test_layout.js` asserts, on any page with more than
one card and at every side-by-side width: identical rendered image size,
air above equal to air below within a pixel, the same air on every card,
and no text passing its image. It failed twice on the way here - at 1024
for the fluid measure, at 390 because the stacked order is tag, title,
image, paragraph and the first check assumed the image came first - and
both failures rewrote the spec before the code.

**The dark card: kept, deliberately.** The bookshelf thumbnail is dark
because the wallpaper is dark, and the card's job is to show what the tool
makes. The app's other finishes are oak and ebonised, both wood; a lighter
render would be a version of the product chosen for the page rather than
the tool's own default, and DESLOP_3D already kept the two demo
screenshots on desktop.html for the same reason. What changed is the
frame: a wallpaper has nothing to lose to a crop, and the 16:9 to 4:3 crop
at full height only lost the sides and kept the demo's near-empty third
shelf, so `crop-top` frames the top two thirds, where the 44 books fill two
shelves. One dark rectangle among six is a fact about one product, not a
palette leak.

## What this brief did not anticipate

- **Every notes page was 282px wide on a phone.** `main.notes` sets its
  own `grid-template-columns` with a class in the selector, which outranked
  the mobile override on plain `main`; so at 390 the notes pages kept their
  34px outer columns inside the 20px padding. Found when the hero's 350px
  phone render came out 282 wide. The mobile rule now names `main.notes`
  too. heat, storage, climate-cost, longevity, skyline, desktop and the home
  page all gain 68px of column on a phone.
- The audits' blindness to panel labels (A3.2), which is site-wide, not one
  figure.
- The thumbnails being upscaled along with the chart (A3.1).
- `climate_allocation_bases.png` shown at 714 from a 1140 render, the
  mirror image of the chart's fault. Left for Part D; it needs a redraw at
  NOTES, not a class change.

## Not done, and why

- The heat and storage `figstyle.py` audits still read `ax.title`. They
  have no panel labels, so nothing is hidden from them today; they are in
  the zips and rebuilding them is a rezip. Part D.
- The library's Method paragraph at the wide edge is 72ch wide by `.flush`;
  it is still a text block at a different width from the cards' captions.
  One edge, two widths. Acceptable on a gallery page.
- `.notes` is now a misnomer on the home page (no notes). Renaming the class
  touches eight pages and the CSS for a word; the comment beside the rule
  says what it means instead.
