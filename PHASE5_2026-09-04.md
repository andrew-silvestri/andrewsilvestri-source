## Part 4. The demand-response bug: investigated, nothing changed

The full report is appended to `ATLAS_DEMAND_RESPONSE_2026-09-04.md`. In
short: the pairing is intentional and the sign convention is right. The
−0.011 response edge was the consumer's only path into its district under
the July engine, which carried influence one way; the fix for fault TWO made
every same-rank edge two-way, so the +0.50 demand edge grew a back-channel
that the engine files in the same fan-in bucket as the response, and
−0.011 + 0.50 wins. Traced on one consumer group: coefficient +0.183, a +1
push moves the district +0.128. With the demand edge one-way the same push
moves it −0.009, correctly signed; with the response scaled to −0.55 it
also works, and that number is chosen to win, so no. Over the six behaviour
scenarios the one-way rule cuts reach by 6 to 45 per cent, moves district
means by under ten per cent, and flips no sign. Recommended, for your
decision: a kind-pair rule in both engines, "district→consumer is one-way",
beside the rank rule, re-verified by the parity test, plus the push-one-
consumer test. Both engines are untouched today.

## Part 4, implemented: property FIVE

**The answer to the question asked first.** Neither a payload flag nor a
kind-pair rule. A per-edge type would be a payload change: the payload is
parallel arrays with no per-edge type, so a flag is a rebuild or a second
hand-patch of a gitignored file. But the payload already says which edges
are responses, by sign: the site's own definition of a negative link is
relief rather than stress. So the rule is stated on that: **a negative edge
within a rank is a response; it is not promoted to two-way, and neither is
the coupling it answers, because the response is that coupling's
back-channel.** Any future response edge inherits it; nothing names
consumers or districts. The other principled candidate, "an explicit
reverse edge overrides the implied back-channel", was measured and
rejected: it also catches the 571 filler-district↔grid pairs, both positive,
and moves 41 of the 60 scenario reaches for pairs whose intent nobody can
verify. The sign rule selects exactly the 1,142 consumer↔district pairs.

**Both engines, together.** `build_throughlines.response_pairs()` and the
`NEG` block in `atlas-app.js`, each with the reason beside it; the app's
comment block gains FIVE under the four faults. `build_scenarios.py`
carried a third copy of the engine and now imports the figures'. Parity
after: 60/60, worst 2.2e-15, step counts equal. HANDOFF §6 has the fifth
property beside the four, with why it exists and what was rejected.

**Two facts the record had wrong, now right.** HANDOFF §6 said the engine
existed twice and insisted the two must agree; it existed three times, and
`build_scenarios.py`'s copy, the one that writes every reach number the
pages show, was never compared to either. The document was wrong about the
fact it was most emphatic about. The third copy is gone (it imports the
figures' engine), and the parity test compares the two that remain. And
`climate-cost/test_scene.js` had been cited as the evidence that the page's
JavaScript engine agrees with `lca.py` while it had never executed: it
needed a module nothing installed, and an unrunnable test looks exactly
like a passing one. It ran for the first time tonight, in Part 5, and
passed.

**The test that would have caught it.** `tests/test_demand_response.py`
pushes consumer groups +1 and asserts each district moves down: 40 sampled
by default, all 1,142 with `--all`; every one moves down, by −0.006 to
−0.010.

**Regenerated, not edited.** `build_scenarios.py --apply` rewrote the
scenario reach fields in the payload (re-recorded in `atlas_payload.json`
with the note extended); `update_atlas_pages.py` now generates the atlas
page's category table and model.html's §6 table from those fields, where
both had been typed, and the §4.4 settle range with them; the throughlines

## Part 5, last item: the model chart's grid

**Measured, both places.** On the page the image sits at 363 to 1077 at
1440, the text column exactly; the page is not the problem. Inside the

**Fixed as a grid.** `build_model_chart.py` states its grid in CSS px of
the 714 canvas: a 95px label gutter on the left of each column (both carry
long y labels), a 239px axes in each, a 30px gap; the donut's legend hangs

key is centred under its panel the same way. Then `sitefig.centre(fig)`,
new, measures the content (every axes' tight box and every legend) and
moves the grid so the margins are equal, for subplot grids and for axes
placed absolutely alike. After: content centred to 0.5px, donut and legend
on one centre, both columns' axes at the same width.

**Every multi-panel figure, surveyed the same way** (a script that
captures each builder's figures and reports axes edges and content
margins). `energy_model_chart.png` was the worst but not the only one:
`climate_allocation_bases.png`, which I redrew earlier tonight with guessed
margins, was 24px off; the throughlines sheet 5px; the longevity
"explained" sheet 4px. The three atlas two-panel figures (11, 14, 08) were
on grids and centred to the pixel. So: the model chart was the only sheet
never put on a grid, and its three faults (the soft display width, the
legend on the panel label, the off-centre columns) have one cause: it was
assembled by hand with a GridSpec's margins guessed, outside the system the
atlas figures use. All four off-centre sheets now call `centre()`.

**The assertion.** `sitefig.grid_problems(fig)`, called from the audits of
every multi-panel builder (throughlines and the model chart, the atlas
figures, the allocation figure, the longevity explained sheet): stacked
panels must share their left and right edges within 3px, or one must span
the other (a full-width row over two columns, the throughlines sheet);
the content must be centred within 3px; a legend hung under a panel must
be centred on it within 3px; axes under 30px wide are colourbars and are
skipped. Every multi-panel sheet on the site passes it, and the model
chart would have failed it on all three counts.
