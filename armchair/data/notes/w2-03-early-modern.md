## E01 Cusa, De docta ignorantia (1440): no centre, Earth moves
- **Locator (verified).** Book II ch. 11 and ch. 12. Source: Klibansky's 1913 Latin edition (IA, `cusa_latin_1913.txt`, noisy OCR; page header "LIBER II 109" falls between the chapters). Ch. 11 says the Earth is not the centre of the world. It also argues that the Earth, since it cannot be the centre, cannot be without all motion. Ch. 12 gives the ship-in-mid-water analogy (motion is judged only against fixed reference points) and calls the Earth a noble star. The Hopkins translation on IA is access-restricted. jasper-hopkins.info refused the connection.
- **Claim.** The universe has no fixed centre or circumference. So the Earth is not at rest and is not the centre. Perceived rest and motion are relative to reference points.
- **Wrong in the same system.** He argues from the theology of the infinite, not from observation. He gives no orbit and no heliocentre. He speculates about the inhabitants of the Sun and Moon (ch. 12). His universe is "interminate" (without bounds), not actually infinite.
- **Event.** Bradley's announcement of stellar aberration: letter to Halley, Phil. Trans. 35, 637-661 (Crossref date 1728; read January 1729, memory/medium). Aberration was the first direct observational proof that the Earth moves. Copernicus 1543 was a theory, not a confirmation. Gap 289 yr. Instrument: Graham's zenith sector, 1725 (memory/medium).
- **Causal line.** None from Cusa to Copernicus or Bradley found. Cusa to Bruno is documented (SEP *bruno*).
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** The claim was asserted and argued, and could have been wrong (the Earth might have been at rest). But it is vague: what moves, how, round what? And it was derived from theology. The "no centre" half matches the cosmological principle only in wording: LOOSE if graded alone. **Counter:** LOOSE overall, because any motion at all satisfies the claim, so it risks almost nothing.
- Confidence: locator high (OCR noisy); event high; grade medium.

## E02 Bruno, De l'infinito, universo e mondi (1584): innumerable suns with planets
- **Locator (verified).** Dialogo terzo (it.wikisource; Gentile ed., *Dialoghi metafisici*, Laterza 1925, pp. 360-389; `bruno_terzo.txt`). Elpino sums up: innumerable suns, infinite earths circling them as our seven circle our Sun. Filoteo, Bruno's spokesman, answers "Così è". Filoteo also explains why the other earths are invisible: they are small, dark and far.
- **Wrong.** The grounds are divine plenitude plus Copernicus, not evidence. His universe is infinite and static. All worlds are animated and inhabited. He says nothing on how many planets or what kind (hot Jupiters were unforeseen).
- **Event.** Mayor & Queloz, Nature 378, 355-359 (Nov 1995; Crossref checked; announced 6 Oct 1995, per w1-02 fetch). Pulsar planets came earlier: Wolszczan & Frail, Nature 355, 145-147, 9 Jan 1992 (Crossref checked). Bruno's claim is about suns, so 1995 (a Sun-like star) is the right event. Using 1992 would give a gap of 408. Gap 411 yr. Instrument: ELODIE spectrograph, OHP (memory: in service 1993/94).
- **Causal line.** None found.
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** It was asserted and specific enough to fail. Other stars could have had no planets, and the Sun-like-star count stayed at zero until 1995. It matches only the existence claim. **Counter:** LOOSE, because the same plenitude argument "predicted" inhabitants everywhere, so the method has no discriminating power. Epicurus made a similar claim.
- Confidence: locator high; grade medium.

## E03 Descartes, Principia III.30 (1644): vortices (MISS)
- **Locator (verified).** Part III art. 30: all the planets are carried round the Sun by the heaven, which turns like a vortex. Source: Bennett tr., earlymoderntexts, p. 47 (`desc_p3.txt`).
- **Event.** Newton, Principia (1687) Book II Sect. IX, Prop. 52-53 with Scholia (Motte 1729 tr., en.wikisource; `newton_b2s9.html`). Newton shows that a vortex's periodic times cannot follow the sesquiplicate (3/2-power) ratio without ad hoc fluid properties. The closing scholium says the "hypothesis of vortices is utterly irreconcileable with astronomical phænomena". Gap 43 yr. "Instrument": mathematical fluid mechanics set against Kepler's third law (1619).
- **Causal line.** Documented. The scholium names the hypothesis it attacks.
- **Grade: WRONG.** It was a confident mechanism for planetary motion and was refuted. **Counter:** Newton's viscous-fluid model was his own construction, and Cartesians (e.g. Johann Bernoulli) kept modified vortices into the 1730s. So the refutation was not accepted at once. That is true, but the brief dates the refuting event, not when people accepted it.

## E04 Descartes, Principia II.36 (1644): conservation of quantity of motion (MISS)
- **Locator (verified).** Part II art. 36 (Bennett tr. `desc_p2.txt`). The quantity of motion is "the product of its speed and its size". God, being unchangeable, keeps the total constant. Direction does not enter (SEP *descartes-physics* §4 agrees: it falls "just short" of momentum conservation). Art. 53 concedes that experience often seems to conflict with his collision rules.
- **Event.** Replies to the Royal Society's call: Wallis, Phil. Trans. 3, 864-868 (11 Jan 1669), and Wren, Phil. Trans. 3, 867-868 (same issue). Huygens, Phil. Trans. 4, 925-928 (12 Apr 1669). DOIs checked on Crossref. They give correct collision rules in which signed momentum is conserved. Worked check (w1-05): masses 1 and 2, speeds +1 and -1, elastic collision. Afterwards the speeds are -5/3 and +1/3. Signed momentum is -1 before and after. Descartes's scalar sum is 3 before and 7/3 after. Gap 25 yr. Instrument: pendulum-collision demonstrations at the Royal Society, 1668 (memory/medium).
- **Causal line.** Documented. Leibniz 1686 (E05) names Descartes's rule. Huygens's collision work began as a critique of Descartes's rules (Blackwell 1977, Isis 68:574-597: existence checked on Crossref, not opened).
- **Grade: WRONG** (the brief allowed MISS or PARTIAL). The quantity as written is not conserved, and the 7 collision rules derived from it are mostly false. **Counter (PARTIAL):** the frame (a conserved total of "mass x speed" in a closed system, grounded in a constancy principle) survived once direction was added. Huygens's and Newton's momentum law is that frame corrected. I grade the claim as written. The frame's survival goes in the page text, not in the grade.

## E05 Leibniz, Brevis demonstratio (1686): vis viva vs energy conservation
- **Locator (verified).** Gerhardt, *Mathematische Schriften* VI, pp. 117-119 (IA `leibnizensmathe10leibgoog`; the OCR page marker 120 follows). Acta Eruditorum, March 1686 (page numbers there unverified). The text says reason requires that the same sum of motive power be conserved: none is lost without being passed on, and none is gained, since perpetual motion fails. Force is measured by the height it can raise. With Galileo's fall law, a body of 1 lb falling 4 ells and one of 4 lb falling 1 ell have equal force but quantities of motion 2 and 4. The m v^2 form and the claim that apparent losses are absorbed by small parts come later (Specimen dynamicum 1695; SEP *leibniz-physics* §3.1-3.2).
- **Decision on event.** Joule, "On the mechanical equivalent of heat", Phil. Trans. 140, 61-82 (1850; Crossref checked). Reason: it is the measurement showing that "lost" mechanical force reappears as heat in a fixed ratio, the empirical core of the claim. Helmholtz 1847 is a theoretical synthesis, not an observation. Joule 1845 was the less precise first paddle-wheel result. Gap 164 yr (Helmholtz: 161; Joule 1845: 159).
- **Causal line.** Documented but indirect. Helmholtz's *Über die Erhaltung der Kraft* (1847; IA `berdieerhaltung00berlgoog`) opens with "Das Princip von der Erhaltung der lebendigen Kraft" (the principle of the conservation of vis viva), which is Leibniz's concept by name. A text search finds no mention of Leibniz.
- **Wrong.** The conserved quantity is mechanical only. There is no heat, chemistry or potential energy as such. He treats vis viva as a rival to momentum, when both are conserved.
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** The core matches: a conserved motive force, measured by work (height times weight), with perpetual motion ruled out. But in 1686 its scope was mechanical, and the extension to heat was a posit without measure. **Counter (STRONG):** the 1695 "hidden in the small parts" claim is the first law in embryo. Reply: it is unquantified and was not in the 1686 text graded here.

## E06 Leibniz to Clarke (1715-16): relational space vs GR 1915
- **Locator (verified).** Leibniz's third paper, 25 Feb 1716, §§4-5 (Bennett tr., `leibclarke.txt`). Space is an order of coexistences, as time is an order of successions. Moving or reorienting the whole world (switching east and west) would make no discernible difference, so absolute space is rejected. Fifth paper §53 (`leibclarke2.txt`): Leibniz *concedes* that true motion differs from mere change of relative place when the cause of the change is in the body.
- **Event.** Einstein's generally covariant field equations, Sitzungsberichte Berlin, 25 Nov 1915 (memory, high). Gap 199 yr.
- **What GR actually does.** The hole argument's "Leibniz equivalence" (SEP *spacetime-holearg* §5) says diffeomorphic models are the same physical world. That echoes Leibniz's shift argument. But the SEP entry treats whether GR supports relationalism as contested (sophisticated substantivalism, metric essentialism, §9). And GR's metric is a dynamical field with its own degrees of freedom: vacuum solutions, gravitational waves. Einstein's later view (Appendix V, 1952, *Relativity*, "Relativity and the problem of space"; unverified, not opened) is that space-time has no existence apart from the field, because it is a structural quality of the field. That puts the field where Newton's space was. It does not reduce space to relations among bodies. Einstein also later dropped Mach's principle (memory).
- **Grade: LOOSE.** The thesis as written (space is *only* relations among bodies) is not what GR says. Making the match needs a retrofit: "bodies" become all fields, metric included. The symmetry argument is the one genuine overlap, and Leibniz himself conceded absolute (cause-based) motion. **Counter (PARTIAL):** Earman and Norton named the principle after Leibniz, and the shift argument is the logical form of the hole argument. That is a real key-respect match, so a fair editor could choose PARTIAL. I keep LOOSE because the claim graded is the relational thesis, not the argument form. Causal line: disputed (Einstein knew the debate; Jammer 1954 foreword, memory; no evidence it shaped 1913-15).

## E07 Maupertuis 1744/1746: least action
- **Locator (verified).** "Accord de différentes loix de la nature…", Mém. Acad. Roy. Sci. Paris, pp. 417-426 (fr.wikisource, proofread 75%). Light takes the path on which "la quantité d'action est la moindre". The quantity of action is the sum of distances times speeds. Maupertuis sides with Descartes that light is faster in denser media. The 1746 Berlin memoir (impact laws, proof of God) was not opened.
- **Decision on event.** No observation confirms least action as such. Its empirical content is that it reproduces Newtonian mechanics. Hamilton says so: Phil. Trans. 124, 247-308 (1834; TCD transcription `hamilton1834.txt`, art. 3). He calls it the "law of least, or as it might be better called, of stationary action". He notes that it only re-derives equations that can be found otherwise, which is why Lagrange, Laplace and Poisson thought little of it. Event: Hamilton 1834, the point where the principle is stated correctly (stationary) as a general method. Lagrange's *Mécanique analytique* (1788) is the other candidate (gap 44). It derives the dynamics from virtual work and treats least action as a corollary (memory). Gap 90 yr.
- **Wrong.** Light faster in denser media (refuted by Foucault 1850). "Least" is wrong. The 1746 derivations were ad hoc. The theological use was decorative.
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** One key respect matches: a single extremal quantity governs light and motion. The quantity and the extremum type were wrong or unproven, and Euler (1744), not Maupertuis, gave the valid mechanical version. **Counter (LOOSE):** with no independent empirical test, the "confirmation" is mathematical equivalence, and Maupertuis's own optical version used a false premise. Causal line documented (Hamilton names the law; the Euler/Koenig affair of 1751-52 is a matter of record).

## E08 Leibniz, Tentamen anagogicum (c. 1696): the "most determined" path
- **Locator (verified).** Gerhardt, *Philosophische Schriften* VII, pp. 270-279 (IA `diephilosophisch0007leib`). On p. 275 the reflected path can be the longest. The ray takes "le chemin le plus déterminé ou unique". Max and min are found by one and the same operation in the analysis of maxima and minima. On refraction he speaks of the path that has no twin in travel time. The date c. 1696 is conventional (memory/medium).
- **Event.** Hamilton 1834, which renames least action as stationary. Gap 138 yr.
- **Causal line.** None found. It is a manuscript essay. The 1707 Hermann letter (Koenig 1751) is of disputed authenticity and was not used.
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** It anticipates the key correction (extremal, not least) with a correct example (concave mirrors). But it is about optics only, gives no action quantity for mechanics, and is justified by final causes. **Counter (LOOSE):** this is Fermat's principle with better calculus, not least action in mechanics. The mechanical claim is where Maupertuis and Euler did the work.

## E09 Kant 1755: nebular hypothesis
- **Locator (verified).** Part II ch. 1, Ak 1:263-267 (korpora.org; `kant1755_259-275.txt`). Elementary matter is spread out at the start (263). Attraction plus weak repulsion deflects falling particles into side-motions and vortices (265). The matter crowds toward one plane, a thin space between two near planes, with particles in free circular orbits (267). The planets form from this layer.
- **Event.** O'Dell, Wen & Hu, ApJ 410, 696 (June 1993; Crossref checked): HST images of protoplanetary disks in Orion. ALMA's HL Tau image (2014; gap 259) shows ring gaps, but the planets there are inferred, not seen. Gap 238 yr. Instrument: HST, launched 1990.
- **Wrong.** Rotation produced by internal repulsion in an initially still cloud breaks angular-momentum conservation. No quantitative treatment.
- **Causal line.** None to 1993. The modern theory descends from Safronov (w1-02, memory).
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** The core picture (rotating flattened disc around a central mass, planets from the disc) was asserted and is now imaged. But its one physical mechanism is wrong, and a disc is the generic outcome for any rotating collapsing cloud. **Counter (STRONG):** the rubric grades the core claim, and the core is exactly what HST imaged.

## E10 Kant 1755: island universes
- **Locator (verified).** Part I, Ak 1:253-256. Kant takes Maupertuis's elliptical nebulous stars (Discours sur la figure des astres). He rejects the idea that they are giant flattened single stars because they would be far too bright. He argues they are systems of many stars, "Milchstraßen", seen at immense distance. Shape (round face-on, elliptical inclined) and faintness fit. He holds their certainty as settled when analogy and observation agree (Ak 1:255). He explicitly leaves the "nebulae properly so called" for observers to test.
- **Event.** Hubble, "Cepheids in spiral nebulae", read by Russell at the Washington AAS/AAAS meeting, 1 Jan 1925 (Discover; one arXiv paper, 1302.0240, gives February 1925, so the date is conflicted). Year 1925 either way. Gap 170 yr. Instrument: Hooker 100-inch (1917) with Leavitt's period-luminosity relation (1912).
- **Grade: STRONG.** The claim matches the 1925 result at its core. It was argued from shape and surface brightness, asserted with confidence, and specific enough to be wrong: Shapley argued the opposite in 1920. **Counter (PARTIAL):** the idea was already in Wright 1750, which Kant knew via a 1751 review (w1-02). His evidence fits gas nebulae equally well. The higher "systems of systems" are speculative. Kant had no distance criterion, and the question swung both ways for 170 years.

## E11 Kant 1754: tidal friction slows Earth
- **Locator (verified).** Ak 1:185-191 (`kant1754.txt`). The ocean's tidal flow presses against the land and the whole sea floor and must slow the rotation. His estimate (Ak 1:188-189): in 2,000 years the year would contain 8.5 hours less rotation. He says corrections enlarge the effect. He found no historical evidence (189). The end state is the Earth turning one face to the Moon, and the same cause explains the Moon's locked rotation (190).
- **Decision on event.** Spencer Jones, MNRAS 99, 541-558 (May 1939; ADS scan `sj1939.pdf`, summary read). The secular accelerations of the Sun, Mercury and Venus are proportional to their mean motions and "can be accounted for by retardation of the Earth's rotation by tidal friction". This is the first decisive *measurement*. Delaunay and Ferrel (1865) proposed the mechanism to explain the Moon's anomalous acceleration but did not measure Earth's slowing independently. Lunar laser ranging (1969) quantified the Moon's recession later. Gap 185 yr (Delaunay: 111). Instrument: telescopic timing of the Sun and planets (Spencer Jones cites 250 years of series; instrument_year 1675 is Greenwich's founding, approximate) plus ancient eclipses.
- **Wrong.** The magnitude: 8.5 h per year after 2,000 years is 30,600 s. The real figure is about 34-46 ms per day after 20 centuries, so about 12-17 s per year. Kant was roughly 2,000 times too high (my arithmetic, from 1.7-2.3 ms per century).
- **Grade: PARTIAL.** The sign, cause and end state are right, and the claim is specific and testable. The single number he committed to was wrong by three orders of magnitude. **Counter (STRONG):** the qualitative law is exactly right, including tidal locking of the Moon, and no one in 1754 could estimate ocean dissipation.

## E12 Kant 1781: geometry known a priori (MISS)
- **Locator (verified).** A24 (Ak 4:32): if space were empirical, it would not be necessary that only one straight line lies between two points. B41 (Ak 3:54): geometric propositions are all apodictic, e.g. that space has only three dimensions. (korpora; `kant_kdrv_A.txt`, `kant_kdrv_B.txt`.)
- **Event.** The Dyson, Eddington & Davidson result, announced 6 Nov 1919. Paper: Phil. Trans. A 220, 291-333 (1920; Crossref checked). The measured deflection (about 1.75") is twice the Newtonian value. In the PPN form (the standard parametrised post-Newtonian framework) the extra half comes from spatial curvature (gamma about 1). So it measures non-Euclidean *space*, not only time warping (standard physics, high). A sharper refutation of A24's own example: gravitational lensing gives two light paths ("straight lines") between two points, first seen in the Twin Quasar, 1979 (memory: Walsh, Carswell & Weymann). Gap 138 yr (publication 1920: 139).
- **Grade: WRONG** on the reading Kant gave: apodictic geometry true of all outer appearances. **Counter:** Kant's thesis concerns the form of intuition, not measured physical geometry. Conventionalists (Poincaré) can keep Euclid and change the physics. The sound part: neo-Kantians retreat to "some metric structure is a priori". Causal line: the eclipse team did not target Kant; uptake came from philosophers (Reichenbach 1920, memory).

## E13 Comte 1835: stellar chemistry unknowable (MISS)
- **Locator (verified).** *Cours de philosophie positive*, tome II (Paris: Bachelier 1835), 19e leçon, pp. 8-9 (IA `bub_gb_eiHRNoN1q88C`). We can determine the forms, distances, sizes and motions of the stars, but "nous ne saurions jamais étudier par aucun moyen leur composition chimique" (we could never study their chemical composition by any means). On p. 11 he says the same of planetary atmospheres.
- **Event.** Kirchhoff, "Ueber die Fraunhofer'schen Linien", Monatsber. Akad. Berlin, October 1859 (reprint Ann. Phys. 185, 148-150, 1860; Zenodo PDF `kirchhoff1860.pdf`). The dark D lines show sodium in the solar atmosphere, and the paper extends the inference to the brighter fixed stars. Gap 24 yr.
- **Instrument note.** The prism spectroscope and the solar D lines existed (Fraunhofer 1814) *before* Comte wrote. What was missing was Kirchhoff's emission-absorption law and a clean flame (Bunsen burner, 1855; memory). The refutation needed a theory, not a new instrument.
- **Grade: WRONG.** Confident (never, by any means), specific, refuted within a generation. No counter of weight. A defender can say Comte meant direct sampling, but the text says by any means.

## E14 Hume 1739: time and empiricist concept criticism vs Einstein 1905 (causal line)
- **Locator (verified).** Treatise 1.2.3 (Gutenberg #4705). The idea of time comes only from succession. Time cannot appear to the mind "alone, or attended with a steady unchangeable object".
- **Event.** Einstein, "Zur Elektrodynamik bewegter Körper", Ann. Phys. 17, 891 (1905; memory, high). It defines simultaneity by an operational procedure. Gap 166 yr.
- **Causal line (documented).** Einstein to Schlick, 14 Dec 1915 (CPAE 8, Doc. 165; English in Physics Today 58(12), 2005, doi 10.1063/1.2169428). He names Mach and "even more" Hume, whose Treatise he studied with passion and admiration shortly before finding relativity. The Autobiographical Notes of 1949 say the same (memory).
- **Grade: LOOSE.** Hume says nothing about clocks, light or simultaneity. The overlap is a method: no concept without an empirical anchor. That method helped Einstein, but it does not anticipate a physical law. **Counter (PARTIAL):** Hume's claim that there is no time apart from observable change is a thesis about nature that relativity's operational time partly bears out. Reply: relativity has a definite physical time (proper time) that Hume's psychology does not supply. This row is best used on the page as a documented-influence case, not as a hit.

## E15 Boscovich 1758: point centres of force vs Faraday 1844
- **Locator (verified).** Theoria, Synopsis Part I arts. 7-10 (Child tr. 1922 of the 1763 ed.; IA `atheoryofnaturalphilosophy`). Matter is points that are "perfectly simple, indivisible, of no extent". Each has inertia and one mutual force that depends on distance: repulsive without limit at short range, alternating, and finally attractive about as inverse-square.
- **Event (as assigned).** Faraday, "A speculation touching electric conduction and the nature of matter", Phil. Mag. 3rd ser. 24 (1844), 136-144 (IA `londonedinburghp24lond`, pp. 140-141). Faraday says the atoms of Boscovich have a great advantage: they are mere centres of force, with the material nucleus reduced to a point ("if I understand aright"). **This is an adoption, not a confirmation.** The brief requires a decisive event, so I flag it. A physical candidate is Rutherford 1911 (the nucleus: matter nearly empty, with repulsive force cores). That would give a gap of 153 and probably PARTIAL. Not adopted without the lead's say.
- **Grade: LOOSE** (Boscovich against the field view Faraday was moving toward). Boscovich's forces act instantly at a distance between points. Faraday's later lines of force are carried by space itself. **Counter:** Faraday explicitly built on Boscovich, so the documented influence is real (causal line documented). But influence is not a match.
