The plastics feedstock that U.S. gas users burned as fuel

For several years, the United States burned a raw material for plastic as fuel. In {{y_est}}, up to {{phi}} per cent of the ethane in U.S. gas went that way, because too few plastics plants and export ships could take it. Ethane left in the gas does not become plastic: it travels with the rest of the gas to its users, is burned in their furnaces, stoves and power plants, and makes the gas hotter. The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) found that in July {{y_heat}} the gas delivered in West Virginia carried {{wv}} per cent more heat than the national average, and in North Dakota {{nd}} per cent more, which it said may indicate ethane left in. It also found that "{{q_stoves}}" (Btu, British thermal units, measure heat). The choice is made at gas processing plants, which can take the ethane out of the gas and sell it as a liquid, mostly to be made into ethylene for plastics, or leave it in. EIA calls leaving it in ethane rejection. But how much was left in? In May {{y_est}} EIA reported estimates of {{lo}} to {{hi}} barrels a day, without saying whose they were. Taken with the year's average recovery, a base this page chose, they come to about {{plo}} to {{phi}} per cent of the ethane in the gas the plants processed. The headline share is an estimate twice over. Plants have taken more of the ethane out since, as plastics plants and export ships took more, but EIA wrote in {{y_rec1}} that it can still be left in when its price comes close to that of gas.

How can a count of barrels show what was left in?

Gas plants produce five liquids (ethane, propane, two butanes and natural gasoline), and when they process more gas they produce more of all five, so a rise in the volume of ethane says little by itself. Its share of the five says more, because EIA identifies ethane as different: "{{q_unlike}}". When plants leave ethane in the gas, its share falls. EIA used the same measure in {{y_28052}}: it reported that the share decreased from {{s2010r}} per cent in 2010 to {{s2015r}} per cent in 2015.

The chart shows ethane's share month by month, which moves by a few points within each year. Below it, ethane and propane production run on scales of their own.

The price, in EIA's words

According to EIA, ethane sold for about the price of natural gas from late 2012 to mid-2017, measured by the heat each carries, and at a premium since. This page found no ethane price series that is free to republish, so it computes no price signal of its own and gives EIA's words instead, at the dates EIA wrote them. The one series that could show when recovery paid is the one series this page could not find free to reprint.

Method

Volumes. The data is EIA's monthly field production of natural gas plant liquids by product for the United States, in thousand barrels a day: ethane, propane, normal butane, isobutane and natural gasoline. EIA also gives their total, and the build checks each month that the total equals the sum of the five. A year's share is the mean of its monthly shares. The share over the latest twelve months ({{ttm_from}} to {{ttm_to}}) is the ethane of those months divided by their total, and growth since {{g_base}} compares the same twelve months with the {{g_base}} average. EIA has also compared ethane with the gas produced: in {{y_ngwu}} it wrote that "{{q_test}}". This page uses the share because it is national and monthly.

The {{y_est}} estimate. On {{d_16191}}, EIA wrote: "{{q_estimates_full}}" (thousand barrels a day). It did not say whose estimates they were. In {{y_est}} plants recovered {{rec}} thousand barrels a day on average, so the range is {{plo1}} to {{phi1}} per cent of the ethane recovered and left in together. Two choices sit under that per cent: a range EIA reported without a source, and a base (the year's average recovery) that this page chose to set it against. It is a reasonable estimate, not a measurement.

Checked against EIA. The build reproduces figures that EIA printed from the same series, among them ethane's share of {{s2010r}} per cent in 2010 and {{s2015r}} per cent in 2015, and {{y2018}} million barrels a day in 2018. The code lists all of them. EIA statements that the series does not reproduce are left off the page.

Limits

This page is a record of what EIA published, not a model. With no price series, it cannot show month by month when the recovery of ethane paid; it can show the volumes and repeat what EIA wrote. The share also moves with the gas itself, which runs wetter or drier from one basin to the next, as well as with the ethane that plants leave in. So it shows the direction of rejection but not its amount, and it cannot show how much ethane is still left in the gas today. In {{y_rec1}}, EIA wrote that "{{q_closer}}". The totals are national, and EIA found rejection "{{q_places}}". A national share does not show clearly where it occurred.
