# Palette trial — four candidate colour schemes

Side task, before Phase 3. You are not choosing anything and neither am I yet.
I want to see the site wearing four palettes so the choice is made by looking
rather than by imagining.

## The four

| # | Name | Swatches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frozen lake | `#6D8196` slate · `#ADD8E6` icy blue · `#FFFAFA` snow · `#000080` navy |
| 2 | Yacht club | `#F2F0EF` off-white · `#BBBDBC` grey · `#245F73` deep blue · `#733E24` brown |
| 3 | Gothic noir | `#000000` black · `#D1D0D0` light grey · `#988686` taupe · `#5C4E4E` dark taupe |
| 4 | Quite clear | `#CBCBCB` grey · `#F2F2F2` off-white · `#174D38` deep green · `#4D1717` maroon |

These are stock palettes from a published list. That is not disqualifying, but
adopting one unchanged is the move the "designed by a person with opinions"
criterion warns about. Treat each as a **starting point to render honestly**,
not as a specification to implement literally. Where a palette cannot carry the
site as given, say so and show the smallest change that makes it work.

## The problem you have to solve first

**Three of these four are light-ground palettes.** The site is dark-ground, and
the figures are not stylesheet objects — roughly thirty PNGs and six MP4s are
generated by Python with dark backgrounds baked in, from hex constants held in
`build_throughlines.py` (`BG`, `INK`, `DIM`, `RULE`, `KCOL`) and the sibling
builders, plus `heat/figstyle.py`, `storage/figstyle.py` and the rest.

Swap the tokens alone and every page becomes paper with black rectangles pasted
on it. I would then be judging four broken renders, which is worse than not
looking at all.

So, for each palette:

- Regenerate **a representative set** of figures against that palette — at
  minimum the home overview chart, one library figure with multiple series, and
  one heat or storage figure. Enough that the pages read honestly. Do not
  regenerate all thirty; this is a look, not a build.
- Where a figure is not regenerated, either leave it out of the preview page or
  mark it clearly as stale so I do not mistake it for the design.
- Say in writing what a full conversion would actually cost: which builders,
  how many figures, how long, and whether the MP4s would need re-rendering too.

The same applies to the JS scenes that draw light-on-dark — `hero-gl.js`,
`margin-scene.js`, `biome-scene.js`, `force-bg.js`. Either retint them or
disable them in the previews and say which you did.

## Contrast — compute it, and expect two of these to fight back

Compute every text/background pair you actually use, as you did for the
baseline. I have run the swatch pairs already and you should reproduce and
extend, not trust me:

- **Frozen lake.** Navy on snow is 15.48:1, fine. But slate `#6D8196` is
  3.88:1 on snow — below body text — and 2.63:1 on icy blue. Icy blue can be a
  surface and never text. So this is effectively a two-colour palette for
  anything readable, plus two surfaces.
- **Yacht club.** The strongest of the four for this site. Deep blue 6.25:1 and
  brown 7.57:1 on the off-white, both pass for body, and they are two genuinely
  distinguishable hues.
- **Gothic noir.** Text works (light grey on black, 13.64:1). But **the palette
  is achromatic — there is no hue in it at all.** This site encodes data in
  colour across dozens of multi-series figures, and taupe against dark taupe is
  2.29:1. State plainly in your write-up whether a data site can use this
  palette without importing a colour from outside it.
- **Quite clear.** Green 8.70:1 and maroon 12.93:1 on the off-white, both
  strong. But green and maroon are **1.49:1 against each other** — near
  isoluminant, and green/red is the most common colour-vision confusion. Fine
  for text, a real problem for adjacent series in a figure. Check it against a
  deuteranopia simulation rather than asserting either way.

Nothing ships that fails AA. If a palette cannot reach AA without modification,
show it modified and label exactly what you changed.

## How to build them

- Base each on the **current corrected `site/style.css`** — post-AA-fix, as it
  stands now. Not on options A, B or C. I want palette to be the only variable;
  structure and type are a separate decision I have not made yet.
- One full sheet per palette: `site/style-palette-1.css` … `-4.css`.
- Previews at `site/_preview/palette-1/` … `-4/`, carrying `index.html`,
  `model.html` and `library.html`. Library matters most here — it is the
  figure-dense page and the one where a palette either survives contact with
  the data or does not.
- `site/_preview/` is already gitignored. Keep it that way.
- Screenshots at 1440×900 and 390×844 into `_deslop/shots/palette/`.

## Write-up

`PALETTE_TRIAL_2026-09-04.md` at the repo root. Per palette: the token mapping
you chose and why, every computed contrast ratio, what you had to change to
make it work, what it costs to convert fully, and — the useful part — **what
this palette does to the site's character.** One paragraph of judgement each,
not a swatch dump. Then a short section on which of the four you would actually
argue for, and whether any of them beats the corrected violet baseline. Say if
none does.
