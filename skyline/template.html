A skyline and a spectrum analyser are the same picture. So the picture is played.

The key

How it sounds

All of this is invented — chosen, not measured. Position across the view picks a note of the city’s chord, lowest on the left. A tower’s height, as a share of this city’s tallest, to the power 1.6, sets how loud its note is; every city’s tallest tower is equally loud. The seven loudest notes sound at once, higher ones tilted quieter; every fourth voice is a triangle wave, the rest sine; a shelf, a low-pass at 3.2 kHz and a limiter sit before the output. No two sounding notes are closer than a minor third. There is no empty horizon on a wheel, so turning changes the chord and never silences it. The line under the meter names the notes sounding now.
