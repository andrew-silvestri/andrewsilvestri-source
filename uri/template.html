Texas and Oklahoma gas customers still pay for Winter Storm Uri

Customers of ten Texas and Oklahoma gas utilities are paying off Winter Storm Uri's gas costs until as late as {{ok_last_year}}. A home pays ${{lo_month}} to ${{hi_month}} a month, and over a third of the total is interest. In February 2021, during the storm, the price utilities paid for gas jumped; the Oklahoma Corporation Commission's order for one utility describes "{{q_100x}}". Passed on at once, the costs would have pushed monthly home bills "{{q_1000}}", the Commission found for Oklahoma Natural Gas. Instead, under laws both states passed in 2021, the costs were financed with bonds, which spread them, with interest, over many years of bills rather than one winter's. Customers of eight Texas utilities and of Oklahoma Natural Gas and Summit Utilities in Oklahoma repay them through a charge on their gas bills. ${{to_util_bn}} billion went to the utilities; customers repay ${{repay_bn}} billion, ${{int_bn}} billion of it interest. So far ${{paid_bn}} billion has been paid; ${{left_bn}} billion is still to come.

Who pays, and how

In Texas, the law sets one charge for all customers: "{{q_tx_uniform}}" of the participating utilities. Future customers also pay it, whether or not they lived there in 2021. Only the utilities' sales customers pay the charge, per unit of gas, inside the gas-cost line of the bill. Customers who buy their gas from other suppliers and pay the utility only for delivery do not pay it. To make collections meet the payments, it is reset at least once a year: {{tx_steps}}. One division's tariff on file is different: Atmos Energy's West Texas division shows ${{tx_wt}} per hundred cubic feet from June 2026, almost twice the others. Its filing does not say why, and this page has not found the reason.

In Oklahoma, the charge is a flat amount on each monthly bill, the same in a cold month as in a mild month. Each class of customer carries a share, which both of the Commission's orders set "{{q_alloc}}". The shares can be reset, and Oklahoma Natural Gas's shares were reset. Its smaller-use homes had {{ong_a_alloc0}} of the total at issue and {{ong_a_alloc1}} in the March 2026 true-up. Both orders exclude customers on the utility's voluntary fixed-price plan in February 2021 and customers in the Low Income Heating Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP). Oklahoma Natural Gas divides homes by yearly use, under or over 50 dekatherms; the dekatherm measures the heat of the gas, not its volume. Oklahoma's average home used {{ok_mcf}} thousand cubic feet a year over {{tx_y0}} to {{tx_y1}}.

Method

Payments. Texas: the official statement's expected amortization schedule by tranche (A-1 ${{tx_a1}} at {{tx_c1}}, A-2 ${{tx_a2}} at {{tx_c2}}). This page computes the interest on the scheduled balance from the closing date, March 23, 2023, on the bonds' 30/360 basis. The first interest payment was on September 1, 2023, and the next payments are on each April 1 and October 1. The check: this method gives weighted average lives of {{tx_wal1}} and {{tx_wal2}} years, which are the lives the statement prints. Also, the balance at December 31, 2025 equals the issuer's report to the dollar. Oklahoma: the Issuance Advice Letters' expected sinking fund schedules, row by row. This page reproduces every interest figure in them from the coupon and the balance to under a cent. What customers repay in Texas: the principal less the reserve, plus the interest, less the interest paid from the proceeds. In Texas, an account funded out of the proceeds paid the first interest payment, ${{tx_prepaid_m}} million on September 1, 2023, before any charge was billed. A reserve of ${{tx_reserve_m}} million, also held back from the proceeds, pays the last instalment. Customers repay both through the principal, so this page does not count them again. The account held six months of interest, about ${{tx_six_m}} million at the coupons, against the ${{tx_prepaid_m}} million that the first payment needed. The statement says that unused up-front funds reduce the first reset of the charge. Thus the Texas total may be up to about ${{tx_unused_m}} million lower. What customers repay in Oklahoma: the principal plus the interest. The reserve funds, ${{ok_reserve_m}} million together, are not netted, because the documents opened for this page do not say how they are used. The reserve funds would change the total by {{ok_reserve_pct}} per cent. Ongoing costs (trustee, servicer, the issuers' expenses) are about ${{ongoing_m}} million a year across the three issues. The same charges pay them, but they are not in the totals above.

A home. Texas: the filed rate in each year from October, then today's rate to the last bill, multiplied by EIA's residential consumption per residential consumer. That consumption is the average over {{tx_y0}} to {{tx_y1}} ({{tx_mcf}} thousand cubic feet a year; {{tx_y1}} alone was {{tx_mcf_last}}). The charge is stated at a pressure base of {{p_tx}} pounds per square inch. EIA counts gas at {{q_eia_base}}. Thus this page multiplies the rate by {{p_eia}}/{{p_tx}}, which gives ${{tx_rate_eia}} per thousand cubic feet. That is the rate CenterPoint's tariff states at {{p_eia}} (${{cnp_1473}} per hundred). Texas Gas Service says its average home pays about ${{tgs_avg}} a month at ${{tx_rate}}. Oklahoma: the filings' own mechanism. This page divides a class's share of the scheduled payments and the stated ongoing costs by the class's customers when the charge was set. The page gives that figure because the charges are set in that way. Today's charge multiplied by the months of the term gives different totals: ${{onga_check}}, ${{ongb_check}} and ${{suo_check}}. They are different because the charge has been both higher and lower than today's. A home's interest share includes the ongoing costs in the amount repaid.

Limits

This page adds up the amounts that the filings fix. It does not follow the payments to the sellers of the gas. It does not compare bonds with other ways the costs could have been recovered; the commissions' orders and staff reports make that comparison. It covers only the gas utilities' bond issues. It excludes gas customers of Texas utilities that did not take part. It also excludes customers who buy their gas from other suppliers and have it delivered (Texas's charge is on sales customers). It does not include the parts of Texas Gas Service's area that its tariff excludes. It does not include Oklahoma utilities whose rates the Corporation Commission does not set, such as city-owned systems, which a separate law covers. The totals include homes only, not the other classes. Payments are as scheduled; collections can be ahead or behind, and the true-ups correct the difference.
